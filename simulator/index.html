Try the analyzer without the hardware

The screen below runs the device firmware's display, touch, and calibration code, ported to the browser. A simulated galvanic sensor feeds it, so readings settle the way a real cell does.

Tap the screen to switch pages · On the calibrate page, hold 1.5 s to sample

Gas at the sensor20.9 % O₂

Sensor output in air10.5 mV

A healthy cell reads 8–14 mV in air. Below 7 mV the firmware flags it as weak. Set it to 0 to unplug the sensor.

Sensor linearity at 100 % O₂100 %

An ageing cell reads low at high O₂ (current limiting). Lower this, then do a 2-point calibration to see how the firmware grades it.

Hardware

The firmware only looks for the ADC at boot, so power-cycle after changing it. Calibration survives a power cycle, as it does in the device's flash.

Things to try

    
    
      
  1. On first boot the status line says NOT CALIBRATED. With the gas set to Air, tap twice to reach the CALIBRATE page and hold the screen. It samples until the reading is stable, then shows AIR SAVED: a 1-point calibration.
    2. 
      
  2. Switch the gas to EAN32. The ring turns orange and the status reads SETTLING while the cell responds, then STABLE once readings hold within 0.15 % for 2 s. The MOD line shows depths at PO₂ 1.4 and 1.6.
    3. 
      
  3. Tap the screen for the detail page: raw millivolts, calibration values, battery voltage, and the calibration type.
    4. 
      
  4. Drop the sensor output below 7 mV and recalibrate to see the weak-sensor warning. Untick the ADS1115 and power-cycle to see NO ADC.
    5. 
      
  5. For a 2-point calibration, after AIR SAVED switch the gas to O₂ and hold again. It captures the O₂ point and shows the linearity: LIN is the O₂/air ratio as a % of ideal, with R² of the fit through zero, air and O₂. Lower the linearity slider to 96 % or 90 % and repeat to see it graded orange or red.
